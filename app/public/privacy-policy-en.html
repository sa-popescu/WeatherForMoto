Motonor privacy policy

Last updated: 23 November 2026

Motonor (formerly MotoMeteo, the WeatherForMoto project) is a personal, free and non-commercial project: a weather and road app for motorcyclists. It has no ads, sells nothing and uses no tracking tools or traffic statistics. This page says what data the app uses, why, and where it goes. The Romanian version is the reference one.

1. Without an account

You can use the app without an account. In that case we keep no data about you on the server. On your device, in the browser's storage (localStorage), only these stay: your favourite places, the last place viewed, the riding profile, the motorcycle's range, the settings (theme, language), the state of the alerts, the route maps downloaded for offline use and a note that you have seen the invitation to install the app. You can delete them at any time by clearing the site's data in the browser.

2. Location

The app reads the device's position only if you allow access to your location, in the browser or in the phone's settings. With that permission, it reads it:

  • when you tap "My location";
  • when the app opens, if access is already allowed or if the "Open on my location" option is on in the "Me" screen;
  • when you come back to the app after at least 15 minutes, if the option is on.

The option turns itself on after you use your location once, turns itself off if you refuse access, and you can turn it off at any time in the "Me" screen. The coordinates are used to get the weather for that place and its name. We do not keep a history of your positions.

When you share a route, the plan holds the stops (names and coordinates), the departure time, the speed and, for a route imported from GPX, the simplified road. A stop taken from the GPS position goes into the plan rounded to about 1 km.

  • a short route, with a few stops, travels whole in the link, in the part after the # sign, which the browser sends to no server, so it does not pass through ours;
  • a long plan, usually one with a road from a GPX file, gets a short link (motonor.bluemouse.cc/s/…): the plan is kept at Cloudflare for 90 days, then deleted automatically. It is not tied to an account, a device or an IP address, and anyone with the link can open it.

If you start sharing your live location during a ride, the phone sends to Cloudflare, about every 30 seconds, the position, the speed and, if you follow a route, the destination's name and the estimated time of arrival. Anyone with the link sees the position and the line ridden. Everything is deleted when you end the ride or stop sharing, and after 12 hours at most. Sharing is not tied to an account; the IP address is used, only as a hash and only for the day, to limit how many shares start each day.

To check how well each weather source got it right, the server keeps a log of what it forecast and what the stations then measured. The log holds only the area rounded to about 5 km and the hour (UTC), with no link to you, your device or your account, and no IP address. It is written at most once an hour for the same area, and records older than 90 days are deleted automatically.

3. With an account

If you create an account, we keep on the server:

  • account data: the email address, the display name (optional), whether the address is confirmed, and the password, only as a hash (it cannot be read back);
  • sessions and codes: the sign-in tokens, the login codes and the reset links, only as hashes and with an expiry;
  • alert preferences: the location for alerts (coordinates and name), the thresholds, the quiet hours and the riding profile;
  • devices for push notifications: the technical delivery address given by the browser and its encryption keys;
  • what you save: saved routes (for a route imported from GPX, its road too, simplified), recorded rides and the hazards reported on the map (place, type, description, duration). Hazards are visible to other riders without your name or email.

You can delete the account at any time from the "Me" screen. Deleting it removes all the data above for good.

4. Security and abuse

To stop attempts to break into accounts and other abuse, we count failed attempts for a short time, by IP address and by email (the email only as a hash). These counters are deleted automatically after one day at most. The server keeps technical logs of the requests (IP address, address requested, time) for 30 days at most, for troubleshooting and security.

When the app runs into an unexpected error, it sends the server a report so we can fix it: the error message, a few technical lines about where in the code it happened, the app's version, the open tab and the type of browser. The report holds no coordinates, account or email address. It goes into the same technical logs (30 days at most), and the first time an error appears in a day it is also sent to us by email.

5. Emails and notifications

The only emails sent are account ones: the sign-in code, the address confirmation and the password reset. We send no weather alerts or marketing by email.

Push notifications are optional and start only after you accept them. They carry weather alerts for the location you chose. You can turn them off in the app or in the browser's settings.

7. Weather sources and maps

For the forecast, our server asks for data for the place's coordinates, without passing on your IP address or account data: Open-Meteo, OpenWeatherMap, MET Norway, Pirate Weather, WeatherXM, Netatmo, the official weather stations through EUMETNET MeteoGate (ANM), the ANM warnings and Meteoalarm, and airport observations (NOAA).

For urban routes with traffic, our server sends TomTom the route's points (the start, the destination and the stops), again without your IP address or account data. On a ride started from the Ride tab, if you leave the route, the app asks the same way for a new route, from your position at that moment to the destination.

Some services are called straight from your browser, so they see your IP address and the area asked for:

  • maps: OpenFreeMap (the base map, with OpenStreetMap data), RainViewer (radar), DWD (the ICON-EU model), EUMETSAT (clouds and lightning from satellite), Open-Meteo (the forecast on a grid). When you download a route's map for riding without signal, OpenFreeMap receives the requests for the map pieces along the route; the map stays only on the phone, until you delete it or for 30 days at most;
  • rain over the next 90 minutes on the "Forecast" screen: RainViewer receives the radar area around the place;
  • place search: Photon (komoot) receives the text searched and the approximate area shown; Open-Meteo Geocoding is used as a fallback;
  • the place name for the GPS position and for the points of a route imported from GPX: OpenStreetMap Nominatim receives the coordinates;
  • routes: OSRM receives the route's points, and Open-Meteo receives points along the road, to find the altitude and the highest point;
  • a ride's story: after you end it, Open-Meteo receives at most 48 points of the ride (rounded to about 100 m) and the day, to find the weather you rode through. The answer is kept only with the ride, on the phone;
  • if our server does not answer, the forecast comes straight from Open-Meteo.

8. Your rights

Under the GDPR, you can ask for access to your data, for it to be corrected or deleted, and for a copy of it. You can delete the account yourself in the app; for the rest, write to us. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with the Romanian data protection authority, ANSPDCP (dataprotection.ro).

9. Contact

For any question about data: weatherformoto@bluemouse.cc

If the policy changes, the date at the top is updated.
